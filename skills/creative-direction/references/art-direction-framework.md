# Art Direction Framework

Use this framework when the visual direction is still unclear.

## A. Context

Identify:

- product
- audience
- market
- trust level required
- complexity of information
- brand maturity
- desired perception
- primary conversion or task

## B. Emotional Target

Choose a small number of meaningful qualities.

Examples:

- calm authority
- engineered precision
- playful confidence
- tactile warmth
- editorial intelligence
- cinematic energy
- technical transparency
- quiet luxury

Avoid long adjective lists.

## C. Visual Thesis

Write one sentence combining:

product character
+
audience expectation
+
visual mechanism

Example structure:

Express the product's precision through an editorial layout built around large technical imagery, restrained typography, and controlled spatial motion.

## D. Dominant Device

Choose the main visual carrier.

Possible choices:

- typography
- photography
- product imagery
- illustration
- data
- motion
- spatial composition
- material surface
- 3D
- video

Supporting devices should remain secondary.

## E. Composition Character

Choose the general compositional behavior.

Examples:

- editorial asymmetry
- strict technical grid
- cinematic full-bleed
- compact product density
- spacious luxury
- modular operational
- spatial layered
- typographic poster-like

Do not use a category as a rigid template.

## F. Typography Character

Determine whether the typography should feel:

- editorial
- technical
- human
- institutional
- expressive
- utilitarian
- luxurious
- compact
- spacious

Then establish scale and contrast accordingly.

## G. Material and Surface

Possible surface character:

- flat
- paper-like
- metallic
- translucent
- matte
- glossy
- dimensional
- soft
- technical
- photographic

Surface styling should connect to the visual thesis.

## H. Motion Character

Choose:

- restrained
- precise
- cinematic
- physical
- elastic
- playful
- immediate
- spatial

The same motion language should recur consistently.

## I. Memorability Test

Ask:

If the logo and text were removed, would the composition still have character?

If not, the direction may still be too generic.

## J. Restraint Test

Ask:

Which element can be removed without weakening the concept?

Remove decorative techniques that do not strengthen the idea.
